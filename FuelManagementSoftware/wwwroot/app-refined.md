# Blockchain Enabled Automated Fuel Dispensing with Real Time Fuel Availability and Routing

## Background of the story

Fuel retailing in developed countries such as the United States, Japan, Singapore, and the United Kingdom has increasingly shifted toward automation and digital payment systems to improve operational efficiency, safety, and customer experience. Contactless payment technologies, RFID-enabled smart cards, and mobile applications allow motorists to pay at the pump without interacting with a station attendant, reducing transaction times and streamlining fuel purchases (RFID Journal, 2022; Gas Tech, 2025). These advanced payment systems have revolutionized the refueling experience by eliminating the need for physical cash handling, reducing queue times at payment counters, and enabling 24/7 self-service operations. The integration of RFID technology has particularly enhanced security and convenience, allowing for seamless authentication and transaction processing. These systems improve accuracy in transactions, minimize human errors, and enhance customer convenience in refueling operations (Smart Card Alliance, 2011). The precision offered by automated systems significantly reduces discrepancies in fuel measurement and billing, while the elimination of manual data entry decreases the likelihood of calculation errors and fraudulent activities. Despite these advancements, most automated fueling systems in developed economies do not provide real-time fuel availability or routing information because fuel shortages are uncommon, making such features unnecessary (Gas Tech, 2025). The stable supply chains and consistent fuel availability in these markets mean that motorists rarely encounter situations where they need to locate alternative stations, rendering real-time availability tracking redundant in such contexts.

In Zimbabwe, the fuel retail sector remains heavily dependent on manual processes, which exposes it to inefficiencies, customer frustration, and revenue losses. Motorists often face long queues, unpredictable fuel availability, and inconsistent service due to the lack of up-to-date fuel information. The manual nature of operations creates bottlenecks at multiple points in the customer journey, from payment processing to fuel dispensing, resulting in extended waiting periods that can span hours during peak times. This problem is particularly severe during tanker offloading periods when fuel stations temporarily close for safety reasons, yet customers frequently arrive unaware of these closures, resulting in wasted time, increased fuel consumption, and traffic congestion. The lack of communication channels between stations and customers means that motorists often travel significant distances only to discover that their intended destination is closed, forcing them to consume additional fuel while searching for alternative locations. The absence of real-time fuel availability and routing information forces motorists to move between multiple stations, aggravating inefficiencies and dissatisfaction (Auditor-General of Zimbabwe, 2016; Auditor-General of Zimbabwe, 2019). This pattern of station-hopping not only wastes valuable time and resources but also contributes to increased traffic congestion in urban areas, as frustrated motorists circle between locations in search of available fuel.

Zimbabwe's continued reliance on paper-based fuel coupon systems further exposes fuel companies to fraud and financial losses. Petrotrade, a major fuel supplier, has experienced significant losses due to coupon duplication, theft, and mismanagement. The physical nature of paper coupons makes them vulnerable to various forms of manipulation, including counterfeiting, unauthorized duplication, and physical theft. For example, a 2025 Newsday report revealed that 59,856 litres of fuel were lost through duplicated Petrotrade coupons involving Zimbabwe Mining Development Corporation officials (Newsday, 2025). This specific incident highlights the systemic vulnerabilities in the current coupon management approach, where internal actors can exploit weak controls to generate unauthorized fuel allocations. Additionally, ZimbabweNow (2023) reported the theft of diesel coupons valued at over US$6,400. Such incidents demonstrate how physical coupon systems create opportunities for both external theft and internal fraud, with significant financial implications for fuel suppliers. Auditor-General reports have consistently highlighted weak internal controls and procurement irregularities in Petrotrade's coupon management systems (Auditor-General of Zimbabwe, 2016; Auditor-General of Zimbabwe, 2019). These recurring audit findings indicate systemic weaknesses that extend beyond isolated incidents, suggesting fundamental flaws in the existing transaction recording and verification mechanisms. These challenges demonstrate the urgent need for secure, transparent, and tamper-proof mechanisms for recording fuel transactions that can prevent unauthorized access, eliminate duplication risks, and provide comprehensive audit trails.

Emerging technologies such as blockchain have been widely recognized for their ability to enhance transparency, traceability, and security in complex transaction systems (Casino, Dasaklis and Patsakis, 2019; Journal of Procurement and Supply Chain Management, 2024). Blockchain technology operates on principles of decentralization, cryptographic security, and immutable record-keeping, making it particularly suitable for applications requiring high levels of trust and verification. By maintaining immutable and verifiable transaction records, blockchain reduces the risk of fraud, improves accountability, and ensures reliable monitoring across supply chains. The distributed ledger architecture ensures that no single point of failure can compromise the integrity of transaction records, while cryptographic hashing makes it computationally infeasible to alter historical transactions without detection. Applying blockchain technology in fuel retail can address the vulnerabilities associated with paper-based coupon systems and provide stakeholders with transparent and tamper-proof records of all fuel transactions. The implementation of blockchain would enable real-time verification of transaction authenticity, create permanent audit trails that cannot be manipulated, and allow multiple stakeholders including fuel companies, regulatory bodies, and customers to verify transaction integrity independently.

Given these challenges, there is a clear need for an integrated digital solution that combines automation, secure transaction management, and real-time fuel availability. The proposed Automated Fuel Management System leverages smart cards and RFID technology to enable automated fuel dispensing, while blockchain provides secure, immutable transaction records to prevent fraud and strengthen revenue accountability. The smart card component eliminates the need for physical coupons by storing encrypted account information and transaction capabilities, while RFID technology enables contactless authentication and rapid transaction processing. In addition, the system incorporates a real-time fuel availability and routing feature, allowing motorists to check which stations have fuel in stock and which are temporarily closed, thereby reducing wasted time and improving refueling efficiency. This routing capability can integrate with mobile applications and web platforms, providing motorists with dynamic updates on fuel availability, estimated queue lengths, and optimal routes to available stations, transforming the refueling experience from a guessing game into a data-driven decision-making process.

Unlike automated fuel systems in developed countries, which generally do not require real-time stock information, the proposed system is specifically designed for Zimbabwe's context, where supply inconsistencies, manual processes, and information gaps create significant operational challenges. The unique market conditions in Zimbabwe, characterized by periodic supply disruptions, infrastructure limitations, and high customer demand relative to available resources, necessitate a more comprehensive solution than those deployed in stable markets. By integrating automation, blockchain-secured transactions, and live fuel information into a single platform, the system aligns with global best practices while addressing local operational realities, improving efficiency, enhancing customer experience, and supporting digital transformation in Zimbabwe's fuel sector. This contextual adaptation ensures that the system not only incorporates international technological standards but also addresses the specific pain points that distinguish Zimbabwe's fuel retail environment from more developed markets.

## Statement of the problem

Zimbabwe's fuel retail sector continues to face major operational challenges caused by manual processes. The reliance on human intervention at every stage of the fuel dispensing process creates multiple points of potential failure, from payment verification to fuel measurement and record-keeping. Customers often travel from one station to another without knowing which sites actually have fuel, leading to wasted time, congestion, and frustration especially when stations temporarily close during tanker offloading. The information asymmetry between fuel stations and customers creates a significant market inefficiency, where motorists must engage in time-consuming and resource-intensive search processes simply to locate available fuel. The absence of a real-time fuel availability and routing system further intensifies these inefficiencies, leaving both service providers and motorists without reliable information for planning, monitoring, and decision-making. Station managers lack visibility into customer demand patterns and competitor availability, while customers operate in an information vacuum that forces them to make decisions based on incomplete or outdated information. This dual-sided information gap prevents optimal resource allocation and creates systemic inefficiencies that affect the entire fuel retail ecosystem.

## Aim

The aim of this project is to design and develop an integrated fuel management system that automates fuel dispensing, incorporates blockchain-based transaction management, and provides real-time fuel availability and routing information. The system seeks to address inefficiencies, revenue losses, and customer frustration arising from Zimbabwe's largely manual fuel retail operations by improving operational accuracy, enhancing transparency, reducing fraud, and enabling motorists to access timely and reliable fuel information for a smoother and more dependable refuelling experience. Through the comprehensive integration of automation technologies, cryptographic security mechanisms, and real-time data systems, the project aims to transform fuel retail operations from a reactive, manual process into a proactive, data-driven service delivery model that benefits all stakeholders in the fuel retail value chain.

## Objectives

- To implement an automated fuel dispensing system with a prepaid smart card functionality
- To integrate blockchain technology into fuel transactions
- To introduce a real-time fuel availability and routing feature

## Research Questions

- **How can fuel dispensing be automated to reduce reliance on manual operations and improve efficiency in Zimbabwean fuel stations?** This question explores the technical and operational requirements for transitioning from manual to automated systems, examining the specific challenges and opportunities present in the Zimbabwean context, and evaluating the potential efficiency gains that automation could deliver to both fuel companies and customers.

- **How effective is a prepaid smart card system in minimizing fuel coupon fraud and ensuring secure transactions?** This research question investigates the security mechanisms inherent in smart card technology, compares the fraud prevention capabilities of digital systems versus paper-based coupons, and assesses the transaction security features that can protect both customers and fuel suppliers from unauthorized access and fraudulent activities.

- **In what ways can blockchain integration enhance transparency and security in fuel transactions?** This question examines the specific blockchain features that contribute to transaction security and transparency, analyzes how immutable record-keeping can prevent fraud and enable comprehensive auditing, and evaluates the practical implementation considerations for integrating blockchain technology into existing fuel retail infrastructure.

- **How can real-time fuel availability and routing information improve customer experience and reduce waiting time at fuel stations?** This research question explores the impact of information availability on customer decision-making, measures the time and resource savings that accurate routing information can provide, and assesses how real-time data can optimize the overall refueling experience for motorists while simultaneously improving station utilization and reducing congestion.

## Relevance of the study

Fuel retailing in Zimbabwe faces challenges such as long queues, manual dispensing, coupon fraud, and unpredictable fuel availability. Customers often waste time during station closures, while companies like Petrotrade lose revenue due to duplicated or stolen coupons. This study proposes an automated fuel station system with prepaid PetroCards and real-time fuel availability, simultaneously improving operational efficiency and customer experience. The research addresses critical pain points that affect multiple stakeholders, from individual motorists seeking convenient refueling to fuel companies struggling with revenue protection and operational optimization.

- **Promotes Operational Efficiency:** Automates fuel dispensing to reduce human error and speed up service. The elimination of manual processes reduces transaction processing times, minimizes calculation errors, and enables continuous operation without the constraints of human operator availability, resulting in higher throughput and more consistent service delivery.

- **Reduces Fraud and Revenue Losses:** Prepaid PetroCards and blockchain-secured transactions prevent coupon duplication and unauthorized fuel access. The cryptographic security features of smart cards combined with blockchain's immutable transaction records create multiple layers of protection against fraud, while the prepaid model ensures that fuel is only dispensed when valid payment has been confirmed, eliminating the risk of unauthorized credit extensions.

- **Improves Customer Experience:** Real-time fuel availability and routing help customers locate fuel quickly and avoid closed or empty stations. By providing accurate, up-to-date information about station status and fuel availability, the system empowers customers to make informed decisions, reduces frustration associated with wasted trips, and creates a more predictable and reliable refueling experience.

- **Supports Accurate Record-Keeping:** Provides reliable data for audits, management decisions, and performance monitoring. The automated nature of the system ensures that every transaction is recorded accurately and immediately, while blockchain technology provides an unalterable audit trail that supports comprehensive financial reporting, regulatory compliance, and strategic decision-making based on accurate operational data.

- **Contributes to Digital Transformation:** Introduces smart technologies in the fuel sector, aligning with national goals for modernization and technological advancement. The implementation of this system represents a significant step toward digitizing Zimbabwe's fuel retail sector, demonstrating the practical application of emerging technologies in addressing real-world challenges and supporting broader economic development objectives.

## Assumptions of the study

This study is based on several key assumptions that guided its development and direction. It is assumed that:

- **Petrotrade will adopt and support the implementation of an automated fuel station system.** This assumption recognizes that successful implementation requires organizational commitment, resource allocation, and strategic alignment with Petrotrade's operational goals and business objectives, including their willingness to invest in infrastructure upgrades and staff training necessary for system deployment.

- **Customers will be willing to use prepaid PetroCards for fuel transactions.** This assumption acknowledges that customer adoption is critical to system success and depends on factors such as ease of use, perceived security, availability of card loading facilities, and customer education about the benefits and operation of the prepaid card system.

- **Fuel stations have the necessary infrastructure (e.g., pumps, internet connectivity) to support automation and real-time monitoring.** This assumption recognizes that the system's functionality depends on existing and upgraded infrastructure capabilities, including reliable internet connectivity for real-time data transmission, compatible fuel pump systems that can integrate with automation technology, and adequate power supply to support continuous system operation.

- **The blockchain and digital systems will function reliably to secure transactions and maintain accurate records.** This assumption acknowledges that the technological components must operate consistently and securely, with minimal downtime, robust error handling, and sufficient redundancy to ensure continuous service availability and data integrity under various operational conditions.

- **Real-time fuel availability data will be updated consistently to ensure accurate routing and customer guidance.** This assumption recognizes that the value of the routing feature depends on the accuracy and timeliness of fuel availability information, requiring reliable data collection mechanisms, prompt update processes, and effective communication channels between station monitoring systems and customer-facing applications.

## Limitations of the study

- **Dependence on Petrotrade's willingness to adopt and implement the automated system.** The success and scope of the research are constrained by organizational factors beyond the researcher's control, including Petrotrade's strategic priorities, budget allocations, change management capabilities, and internal decision-making processes that may affect the pace and extent of system implementation.

- **Potential technical challenges, such as network connectivity issues or system malfunctions.** The study acknowledges that technological systems are subject to various technical risks including network outages, hardware failures, software bugs, and compatibility issues that could affect system performance, data accuracy, or service availability, potentially impacting research findings and system evaluation.

- **Customer adaptation to the new technology may be slow or inconsistent.** The research recognizes that technology adoption is a gradual process influenced by factors such as digital literacy, trust in new systems, resistance to change, and learning curves, which may result in varying levels of customer engagement and utilization that could affect the measured outcomes and benefits of the system.

## Delimitations of the study

- **The study focuses only on selected Petrotrade fuel stations in Harare, excluding stations in other cities or rural areas.** This geographic limitation allows for focused research within a manageable scope while acknowledging that findings may not be directly generalizable to stations in other urban centers or rural locations that may have different infrastructure capabilities, customer demographics, or operational contexts.

- **The system is designed specifically for fuel dispensing, prepaid smart card usage, blockchain transaction recording, and real-time fuel availability and routing, excluding other management areas such as HR, maintenance, or inventory control.** This functional delimitation ensures that the research remains focused on core fuel retail operations while recognizing that comprehensive fuel station management involves many other functions that are beyond the scope of this particular study.

- **The study considers Petrotrade as the primary case organization, and does not compare detailed operations of other fuel companies in Zimbabwe.** This organizational focus allows for in-depth analysis of a single organization's implementation while acknowledging that other fuel companies may have different operational models, challenges, or requirements that are not addressed in this research.

- **The study focuses on customers using prepaid smart cards or digital payments, excluding cash-only transactions.** This payment method limitation reflects the system's design focus on digital payment mechanisms while recognizing that cash transactions represent a significant portion of current fuel retail operations that would require separate consideration and potentially different system features.

- **The blockchain integration is deliberately limited to transaction recording for fuel purchases, without implementing advanced features like smart contracts or third-party integrations.** This technological delimitation focuses the research on core blockchain functionality for transaction security and transparency while acknowledging that blockchain technology offers additional capabilities such as automated contract execution and external system integration that could be explored in future research or system enhancements.
